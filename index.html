A new name for 2026
IgniteCS→IgniteAI

When this Expo began, learning to code was the way into technology. That has changed. AI now writes much of the code — so the hard part, and the exciting part, is knowing which problem is worth solving, and understanding it well enough to solve it.

Today biologists, musicians, designers and founders build with AI right alongside programmers. Our name now says so. Same Expo, same standards, same community — open to every kind of builder.

Official Rules

The fine print.

Eligibility

Student

Each applicant must be a student in an educational institution and enrolled in grades K–12 at the time of qualification. Students participate in different divisions based on their grade level: Grade K–3, Grade 4–6, Grade 7–8, and Grade 9–12 Senior Division. The division for team projects with students in different grades is determined by the highest grade represented.

Limited to One Entry

Each student is limited to one entry per year. Teams may consist of up to five participants. Team projects compete with individual projects in the same categories. Keep in mind that for team projects, the judging panel will have higher expectations commensurate with the number of students on the team.

Projects must be produced by the participants and the participants only. If a project submitted under an individual's name is discovered to have been prepared by a team of more than one student, the participant will be disqualified.

New Project

Students are allowed to enter the IgniteAI Expo using projects submitted to any other science fairs or competitions.

However, any project re-entered from a past IgniteAI Expo must show substantial new development and extension to be eligible for an award. Final determination shall be made by the category judges.

Supervision

Students not accompanied by their own parent(s) must be under authorized supervision by an adult escort, teacher, or administrator. The IgniteAI Expo is not responsible for the supervision of any participant.

Application Acceptance Criteria

Submission of an application to the IgniteAI Expo does not guarantee acceptance. The Expo can reject applications on the basis of inappropriate content or for violations of Expo regulations.

Students, parents, and advisors should be aware that these acceptance criteria are not intended to limit the number of participants but rather, by requiring higher standards for project abstracts, to improve the quality of the Expo and to ensure that all participants are able to effectively communicate their projects to the judges.

The official list of acceptance criteria

  1. Applications without a Project Summary will be rejected without recourse to appeal. A team submits a single application for the whole team, listing every member, with one contact email.
  2. Projects may be based on existing ideas and examples, but students must add their own new extensions and improvements. Projects that merely follow existing tutorials, code, or lessons are generally not acceptable.
  3. The project must be a working, technology-driven build, implemented using programming, AI, data, or hardware techniques. You are required to show a live demo of the project during your interview, and be able to explain the implementation details to the judges.
  4. Acceptance to present a project at the IgniteAI Expo requires the approval of an application submitted by the student(s) or their parent(s)/guardian(s).
  5. Application fees are not refundable. The only exception is for multiple payments for the same application.
  6. Applications may be rejected for failing to satisfy the rules of the Expo.
  7. Project Summaries must communicate ideas effectively and use standard English.
Do I need to be a strong programmer?

No. Students enter at every level, from a first Scratch project to original research, and are judged within their own grade division. What matters: it works, you understand it, and you can show it live.

My passion isn't computer science. Is this for me?

Yes — that's the point of AI + X. Bring your biology, music, economics, history, sports or art, and enter whichever track fits what you built.

I live outside the United States. Can I take part?

Yes. The Expo is entirely online. Applications and interviews are in English, and this page shows every date in your local time.

Our Roots

The Expo grew out of Google's IgniteCS program, founded in 2015 to connect university mentors with K–12 students, particularly in underrepresented communities. That mission hasn't changed: give every young innovator a stage.
